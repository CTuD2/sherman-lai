Research & Practice

An integrative approach to Chinese medicine

A historical record of Lai's clinical background, research presentations, and the philosophy behind his (now retired) practice.

Sherman Lai retired from clinical practice in 2023 and the Centre of Integrative Natural Medicine is no longer operating. This page is a historical record of his professional and academic activity. It is not medical advice, not an offer of treatment, and not a claim that any therapy, formula, or protocol cures, treats, or prevents any disease. Anyone with a health concern should consult a licensed, currently practising healthcare provider.

Clinical background

Lai practised acupuncture and Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) in Guelph from 1986 to 2023, and wrote and presented on TCM theory and clinical observation at Canadian and international conferences, including meetings co-sponsored by the World Health Organization. His practice centred on Traditional Chinese Medical theory: the idea that illness reflects an imbalance in the body's energetic and physiological systems, and that treatment should address the underlying pattern of imbalance rather than a symptom in isolation. Over his career the Centre of Integrative Natural Medicine treated a wide range of chronic and acute conditions — including pain, digestive disorders, arthritis, sleep disorders, and hormonal imbalances — and, working alongside oncologists and other treating physicians, served as a complementary resource for cancer patients rather than a replacement for conventional oncology.

He cited peer-reviewed research, including University of Toronto studies on acupuncture's physiological effects, in explaining how acupuncture is understood to stimulate the body's own pain-modulating and anti-inflammatory responses. He was also a long-standing advocate for professional standards in acupuncture and TCM, contributing his clinical experience to Ontario's regulatory process for the professions in 2003–2004.

Research papers & conference presentations

Over the course of his career, Lai authored and presented the following clinical observation reports and literature reviews at TCM and acupuncture conferences in Canada and the United States. These are case-series and clinical-observation write-ups reflecting Lai's own practice, not independent peer-reviewed or placebo-controlled trials:

  • 1995Clinical Observations: A Chinese formula for the treatment of pain and associated symptoms of cancer (34 cases) — presented at the World Association of Chinese Medicine conference, Toronto
  • 1996The treatment of musculoskeletal pain — a comparative clinical study of acupuncture versus acupuncture and cupping therapy — presented at the Fourth World Conference on Acupuncture, New York (co-sponsored by the World Health Organization)
  • 1996Clinical Observation: A Chinese herbal formula for the treatment of pain and associated symptoms of cancer (56 cases) — presented at the same WHO-co-sponsored conference in New York
  • 1997Clinical Observation: A Chinese herbal formula, OA Plus, for the treatment of osteoarthritis (105 cases) — later used as a practitioner reference handbook
  • 2003Chinese herbal formula for the treatment of viral and bacterial infections (800 cases)
  • 2003An all-natural Chinese herbal remedy for the relief of stress, anxiety, depression and sleeping disorder (1,000 cases)
  • 2004An all-natural traditional Chinese herbal formula for prostate care (68 cases)
  • 2004A Chinese herbal formula for the intervention of cancer (58 cases)
  • 2004A traditional Chinese herbal formula for the intervention and inhibition of cancer metastasis (11 cases)
  • 2005An integrative Chinese herbal formula for the therapeutic treatment of menopausal symptoms caused by Yin and Yang deficiency of the kidneys with liver fire
These titles are presented as a historical bibliography of Lai's own case-series reports, not as evidence that any formula treats or cures cancer or any other disease. None of the products or formulas described are currently manufactured, sold, or available — BioHerbalai Inc., which produced them, ceased operating in 2017.

Practice philosophy

Lai often described his approach as treating "the root, not just the branch" — using TCM diagnostic frameworks such as Qi, blood, and organ-system balance alongside dietary and lifestyle guidance, in coordination with a patient's other medical care rather than as a replacement for it. He was a frequent point of reference for Western practitioners seeking to understand TCM's diagnostic logic, and spoke at medical institutes on the relationship between TCM theory and Western clinical science. Following his retirement, he has continued that educational role internationally, including collaborating with practitioners in Taiwan, Japan, and China on integrating Chinese and Western approaches to cancer care.
